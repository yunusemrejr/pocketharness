# Media on the web (video · audio · images · sourcing)

The rule that organizes everything: **media is the most expensive and the most fragile pixels on the page** (the bytes, the decode, the autoplay policy, the license). A hero video that *feels* premium but costs 8s of mobile load and fails in 2 browsers is a negative. The doctrine: *the still image beats the video unless the video is doing a job the still can't*; *nothing autoplays with sound, ever*; *every piece of media has a license you can point to*. Sources: web.dev perf rules, MDN autoplay policy, the stock-site license terms (Unsplash/Pexels/Pixabay/Wikimedia CC), Google's Image Optimization guide.

## The decision table (what medium, when)

| Job | Default | Upgrade to | Only when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero "product" | **the real product screenshot** (or a short muted loop of it) | the 10-20s muted loop | the motion *is* the message (a video/product-of-motion) |
| "How it works" | 3 static frames + captions | scrollytelling frames (`storytelling`) | the *change over time* is the point |
| Atmosphere/brand | one strong photo (a duotone/on-palette) | the subtle loop (ambient) | the brand is *film* (a creator tool, not a SaaS dashboard) |
| Team/proof | real photos (or nothing) | — | the AI face is the *instant-untrust* (`copywriting`) |
| Illustration | the 2-color on-palette, hand or vector (`custom-svg`) | the 3D (a Blender/Spline — `threejs`) | the style is *the* brand |
| Texture | a CSS pattern / a small tiled texture (`css-battle`) | — | never a photo "texture" (the bytes don't add to the feel) |
| Demo | the GIF (small region only) / the Lottie | the video | the region is < 400×250; else the video |

**"None" is a valid choice** — the type + whitespace + one accent is the Linear/Stripe mode; a page needs media to *fill* a vacancy in the design, the design is the disease. The anti-test: *remove the hero media — does the page get better?* (for most SaaS it does, and the "but it felt premium" is the LCP you just bought back).

## Images (the daily driver — the full pipeline)

- **Format**: **AVIF** (best size/quality, ~30-40% under WebP) → **WebP** (the fallback, universal) → **JPEG** (photos, the JPEG-only-safe floor) → **PNG** (only true transparency — a "PNG-24" photo is a bytes crime) → **SVG** (every vector/illustration/logo — the `custom-svg` + `svg-assessment` skills). The delivery: `<picture>` with the AVIF source + the WebP + (the last res sort: `srcset` + `sizes` per breakpoint (the 390/768/1200/1600 set) — the *art direction* `srcset` (different crops per width) for the hero (the mobile crop is face-safe, the desktop crop is the wide).
- **Size the file to the slot**: the rule of thumb **~100-200KB per image for a hero (AVIF ~60-120)**, ~30-80KB for a card grid image, ~10-30KB for an avatar/icon-photo. The 800KB hero JPEG is the LCP killer — *the hero image is the LCP element in most landing pages* (`web-performance`): lazy-load everything *below the fold* (`loading="lazy"`), eager + `fetchpriority="high"` + (the `preconnect` to the image host) for the *one* above-fold hero, `decoding="async"` on the rest; the **`object-fit: cover` + the known aspect-ratio** (the `aspect-ratio` CSS / the width+height attributes — the CLS killer: an image that resizes the page as it loads is the layout-shift incident — *always* set the dimensions, the `aspect-ratio: 16/9` reserve is the rule).
- **The optimization stack** (the pipeline, in order): *crop to the slot's aspect first* (the subject-safe: the face/eyes — `object-position: center 30%` for portraits is the classic), *downscale to the max rendered width × 2* (the Retina cap — a 4000px photo rendered at 800px is a 25:1 waste; 1600px is the ceiling, 800-1200 is the typical hero), *compress* (the AVIF/WebP quality ~70-80 for photos — the perceptual floor, not the 100), *strip the metadata* (the EXIF — the GPS in a product photo is a *privacy* leak as well as a kB; `exiftool -all=`, `sharp`, `squoosh`), then *the pipeline*: `next/image`/`satori`/the CDN resize (`?w=800&f=avif`) — **the CDN-resize is the production answer** (the OneFetch: `https://cdn.example.com/img/w_800.avif` — the cache per variant, the format negotiation, the never-uploaded-4000px rule).
- **The hero's special rules**: the *first* paint matters → the `fetchpriority="high"` + the `preload` (the `<link rel=preload as=image>` — the explicit, for the non-`<img>` heroes (the CSS-background hero is the *lazy* one (the CSS-bg is not in the critical path the way an `<img>` is — the `background-image` hero is the "why is my LCP 6s" classic; *use an `<img>` with `object-fit` for the hero, not the CSS-bg* — the one exception where the "modern" approach is the slower one), the *poster* (the still frame the video swaps to, below), the *gradient placeholder* (the `onload` fade-in *from a blurred/tiny version* (the blur-up: the 20px AVIF (a ~2KB) that upscales-blurred into the full — the *perceived* load (the perceived > the real, the `web-performance` rule).

## Video (the high-cost, high-reward — the rules are the law)

- **The autoplay law (MDN spec, all modern browsers)**: **autoplay is allowed only if (a) muted AND (b) the user has interacted with the domain before (the "user-engagement" heuristic) OR the video is a `media` with the `playsinline` + (the iOS rule: `playsinline` is *mandatory* (the iOS-fullscreen-on-autoplay override kills the "background loop" (the `playsinline` attribute is the iOS fix (the `muted` + the `autoplay` + the `loop` + the `playsinline` are the *4 attributes of the web-background-loop* — the missing `playsinline` is the *iOS-locks* bug (the video does *nothing* on iOS (the *the most common "my video works on my Mac, not on my iPhone"* find))); the *sound is never autoplayed* (no browser allows it; the "autoplay with sound" attempt = the *blocked* + the *the user's silence is the default* (the *muted-start with a visible un-mute* is the pattern for the *narrative* video (the YouTube-embed's the default (the *YouTube's* muted-embed? no — the *YouTube's* autoplay-with-sound is *blocked* in iframes (the `allow="autoplay"` on the iframe is the *embed* permission (the *`<iframe allow="autoplay; encrypted-media">` is the *YouTube's* the autoplay-permission (the *the embed's the *muted* param (`?autoplay=1&mute=1`) + the `allow="autoplay"` (the *4 lines of the YouTube-embed-that-autoplays* (the `mute=1` + the `autoplay=1` + the `allow=autoplay` + the `playsinline` — the *missing one* = the *the silent "video that never starts"* (the *the 4th-attribute bug*).
- **The poster (non-negotiable)**: `<video … poster="frame.jpg">`. No poster = a black rectangle until play; the poster *is* the video's pre-play state, i.e. its "image". Pick a frame that says what the video is, not just frame 1.
- **Captions**: `<track kind="captions" src="subs.vtt">` (WebVTT) + a transcript line. Accessibility is the floor; more practically most viewers watch **muted** — if the video carries a message, the message must survive with the sound off (in that case captions are *content*, not garnish).
- **Demo clips (GIF vs video vs Lottie)**: GIFs are dead for anything but tiny regions — no audio, no seek, ~2-4× the bytes of an equal MP4, 256-color banding. Short *footage* demos: small MP4/WebM loops. *Illustrated* motion: **Lottie/Rive** (vector JSON — `animation-libraries`) — a 30KB Lottie beats a 3MB demo GIF every time.
- **Delivery**: < ~10-15MB → self-hosted MP4 (plus a WebM `<source>`), **`preload="metadata"` never `auto`** (auto is mobile-data theft for video nobody watches); big/long → a streaming CDN (Mux, Bunny, Cloudflare Stream) with HLS; `#t=5` seeks for demos.
- **Ambient background loops**: the 4 attributes `muted autoplay loop playsinline` (missing `playsinline` = the "works on my Mac, not on my iPhone" bug), `poster`, `opacity: .4–.7` under a scrim, 10-20s, *product motion* (not stock nature), and honor `prefers-reduced-motion` (pause to still). **The still image is the default** — the loop must be earning its 2MB.

## Audio (the smallest surface, the strictest law)
- **Never autoplay with sound** — no browser allows it, and the attempt is both blocked and hostile. Sound starts from a user gesture (the play button, the tap); the sound-on/off toggle is the pattern for ambient loops.
- `<audio>` for pure sound (voice memos, jingles): `preload="none"`, a custom control where the brand matters (the raw native controls are the ugly tell), volume user-controlled (never push past ~100%).
- If the "audio" is really a reaction sound (success chime), ask whether it's *wanted* — `copywriting`'s product-voice rule: default-off, toggle in settings, never surprise.

## Sourcing media online (the license part people actually get wrong)
The standing rule: **if you can't name the asset's source + license in 30 seconds, the asset gets removed.**
- **Unsplash / Pexels / Pixabay**: free commercial use, attribution **not required** (a credit is courtesy + PR, not law). The prohibitions that actually bite: (a) **no reselling/distributing the images themselves** ("a pack of 50 photos" as your product = no); (b) **no implied endorsement** — real brands/logos in the photo reading as "they recommend us"; (c) **no identifiable person posed as your customer/employee** (the stock-person "testimonial" violates the license *and* is a trust-killer — `copywriting`).
- **CC-licensed** (Wikimedia Commons, Flickr CC…): the license is **per file**. CC0 = public-domain-ish (safe). CC-BY = **attribution mandatory** (creator + title + license link, discoverable — the credits page counts). CC-BY-SA = BY + share-alike — a modified BY-SA image inside your UI can license-implicate the *derivee*; check before cutting it.
- **Paid stock** (Adobe Stock, Getty, Shutterstock): the subscription is not "own forever" — read each license: **editorial vs commercial** (Getty editorial = news coverage, *not* your product/ads), per-use vs royalty-free scope, no logo use, no merch.
- **Hotlinking** (linking straight to their CDN): default **no** — you burn *their* bandwidth, the image can vanish tomorrow, and many CDNs' ToS forbid it. Download + rehost, then run the pipeline above.
- **AI-generated**: generated *product/abstract* imagery is fine (nobody can tell, the product is the truth); generated *humans* are an instant tell (people can tell, and the uncanny shows). Check the generator's terms (commercial rights usually yes; "don't prompt to imitate a living artist's style" usually yes).
- **The credit block**: one footer line — "Photos: Unsplash — A. X, M. Y" with links. Cheap, professional, defensible in the legal email.

## The failure index (symptom → cause → fix)
- *Hero is gorgeous, LCP is 7s* → JPEG-4000px, no `fetchpriority`, CSS-background hero → AVIF/WebP at slot size, an `<img>` not a bg, `fetchpriority="high"` + `<link rel=preload>`.
- *Video works on my Mac, not on my iPhone* → missing `playsinline` or unmuted autoplay → the 4 attributes: `muted autoplay loop playsinline`.
- *40MB loads before a 6s clip* → self-hosted progressive MP4 → HLS/CDN (Mux/Bunny/Stream) or `ffmpeg -crf 28 -preset slow` compression.
- *Layout jumps when images load* → missing dimensions → `width`/`height` + `aspect-ratio` on every slot (the CLS bug).
- *Image grid is a size mess* → mixed aspects, natural sizing → fixed grid aspect + `object-fit: cover` + per-slot crop.
- *Legal asks who owns that photo* → untrackable asset → the 30-second rule: no source + license in 30s = remove it.
- *"Our brand video autoplays with sound!"* → no browser does that — it's a YouTube `?autoplay=1` without `mute=1` (or an un-muted MP4) → mute or drop the autoplay.
- *Page feels heavy, no single big asset* → the accumulation of small unoptimized images → per-image budgets (hero ~60-120KB AVIF, cards 30-80KB) + CDN resizing.
- *The AI hero looks "off" and nobody can say why* → a generated human (the uncanny) → swap to a real product shot or abstract; the tell is the *person*.
