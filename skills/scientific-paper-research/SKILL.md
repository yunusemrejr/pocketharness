# Scientific paper research

Use for literature searches, paper comparisons, evidence reviews and reproduction planning. Define the research question, population or system, outcome, date range and inclusion criteria before collecting citations. Distinguish a quick technical scan from a systematic review; do not claim exhaustive coverage without a reproducible search and screening process.

Read [discovery and identity](references/discovery-identity.md) for scholarly APIs, deduplication, versions and corrections. Read [critical reading and replication](references/critical-reading.md) to evaluate methods, quantify claims and reproduce results.

Search results and abstracts are discovery aids. Read the actual paper, methods, tables and relevant supplements before making detailed claims about experiments. Track whether the source is a preprint, accepted manuscript or version of record. Cite the exact version inspected and check for corrections, withdrawals and retractions when they affect the task.

Separate reported results from your calculations, interpretation and unanswered questions. Preserve units, sample sizes, uncertainty, comparison baselines and experimental conditions. A benchmark result does not establish general superiority, and a statistically significant association does not automatically establish causality.

Return a claim-to-source synthesis, contrasting evidence and concrete limitations. For replication, specify data, code, dependencies, seeds and the measured acceptance criterion. Label access-limited papers and unverified claims explicitly instead of fabricating details from metadata or snippets.
