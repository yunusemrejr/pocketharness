# Critical reading and replication planning

Read the paper in the order needed for the question, usually starting with the claimed contribution, methods and decisive results rather than the discussion's interpretation. Record the exact hypothesis, unit of analysis, sample construction, inclusion criteria, comparator and outcome. Identify whether the design is experimental, observational, simulation-based, theoretical or a benchmark study. Each design licenses different conclusions; do not infer real-world effectiveness from a synthetic benchmark without evidence.

Create a claim ledger containing the authors' claim, supporting table or equation, assumptions, uncertainty and your assessment. Inspect denominators and exclusions behind headline percentages. For empirical comparisons, record sample sizes, train/validation/test construction, repeated runs, confidence intervals or other uncertainty estimates, and whether comparison methods received similar tuning budgets. Multiple measurements from the same participant, site or document family may not be independent observations.

Distinguish effect size from statistical significance and practical importance. A p-value is not the probability that the null hypothesis is true. Confidence intervals depend on the specified procedure and assumptions. If the design includes many endpoints or repeated analyses, inspect multiplicity handling and preregistration where relevant. Missing reporting should remain a limitation rather than an accusation or a guessed implementation. Use discipline-appropriate guidance to assess reporting completeness in clinical or health research; this does not replace a separate risk-of-bias assessment. [EQUATOR reporting guideline library](https://www.equator-network.org/reporting-guidelines/).

For machine learning, look for leakage through preprocessing, repeated benchmark use, retrieval corpora, hyperparameter search, near duplicates or test-time access. Reconstruct the actual inference budget, including tool calls, rerankers, retries and ensembles. Parameter count alone is not a fair comparison of inference cost. Separate an ablation that removes one factor from a comparison that changes several factors simultaneously. Verify whether error bars reflect random seeds, data sampling or independent experiments.

For quantitative reproduction, first reproduce one decisive quantity from the paper using a small independently checked example or disclosed raw data. Track code commit, dataset version, licenses, environment, numerical precision, random seeds and hardware. Reproduce preprocessing and evaluation exactly before proposing improvements. Define a reasonable tolerance for stochastic or floating-point differences and distinguish reproduction of a reported number from independent replication of the underlying finding.

Check equations dimensionally and inspect limiting cases. An implementation matching a published equation can still expose an inconsistency in the paper; document the discrepancy without silently correcting the target. If code is missing, label reconstructed choices and run sensitivity analyses around them. End with a concise evidence judgment: what was directly verified, what remains conditional, which alternative explanations survive and what next experiment would most efficiently distinguish them. Citation accuracy and methodological caution should strengthen the answer without hiding the practical conclusion in generic disclaimers.
