# Natural Editorial Writing: patterns and examples

## Draft from substance
Before styling, list what the reader needs to know, what evidence supports it and what action follows. Missing facts stay missing; do not add anecdotes, quotations, customer stories or statistics to make prose sound human. Preserve uncertainty and technical qualifiers that change meaning. If a user supplies voice examples, infer sentence length, directness and formality without imitating a person's private identity.

Before: “In today's rapidly evolving landscape, our cutting-edge solution empowers teams to unlock unprecedented productivity.”
After, if supported: “The tool groups duplicate bug reports so the support team can review one issue at a time.”
The second sentence works because it states a concrete behavior, not because particular fashionable words are forbidden. If the product does not actually group reports, neither sentence is acceptable.

## Structure and voice
Lead with the point the reader came for. Let each paragraph develop one idea and connect to the next. Use examples where they resolve ambiguity. Lists help parallel items or steps; they should not fragment every thought. Headings should describe content, not provide a decorative label for every two sentences. Vary rhythm naturally while retaining the user's preferred level of detail.

Avoid repetitive question-answer hooks, inflated praise, empty contrast constructions, invented labels and automatic grand conclusions. Do not introduce typos or forced informality to appear human. Technical documentation may need repeated terms for precision; replacing every occurrence with a synonym can make it worse. Marketing copy may be expressive, but claims still need support.

## Editing pass
Check names, dates, units, scope, links and causal claims. Remove sentences that neither inform, explain nor move the reader toward the requested action. Read the result aloud or inspect awkward transitions, but do not mistake smoothness for truth. Preserve required legal/product wording and clearly distinguish suggestions from quoted text. For translations, preserve meaning and register rather than translating idioms literally. Completion means a usable draft with material unknowns disclosed, not a claim that it will fool a detector or sound universally human.
