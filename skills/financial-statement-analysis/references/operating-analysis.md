# Operating performance and cash conversion

Build an analytical bridge before interpreting a ratio. Define revenue, operating profit, net income, operating cash flow, capital expenditure, debt and cash using the issuer's presentation. Distinguish GAAP or IFRS figures from management-defined adjustments. For an adjusted measure, show the reported starting point and every reconciling item. An exclusion recurring every year should not silently disappear from the underlying economics. US non-GAAP presentation requirements are jurisdiction-specific; consult current guidance for regulated disclosures. [SEC non-GAAP guidance](https://www.sec.gov/corpfin/non-gaap-financial-measures).

Gross margin equals gross profit divided by revenue only when cost-of-sales presentation supports that comparison. Operating margin uses operating income, not net income. A growth percentage with a negative or near-zero denominator can be misleading: show the absolute change and explain the sign rather than treating a large percentage as ordinary growth. Separate organic growth, acquisition effects and currency translation when disclosed; unknown decomposition remains unknown.

For working capital, use a stated convention. Days sales outstanding often uses average receivables divided by compatible credit sales times days in the period; total revenue is a labeled proxy when credit sales are unavailable. Inventory days use cost of sales, while payable days ideally use purchases. Substituting cost of sales for purchases changes interpretation. A cash conversion cycle is DSO plus inventory days minus payable days, but mixed periods or point balances can overwhelm apparent precision.

Inspect the bridge from net income to operating cash flow. Separate noncash adjustments from changes in receivables, inventory, payables and other operating balances. Rising cash flow driven by deferred supplier payments differs from improving collections. Factoring, supply-chain finance, acquisitions, divestitures and currency changes may break simple balance-difference explanations. Reconcile to notes rather than assuming every change represents operating cash movement.

Free cash flow has no single universal definition. State whether it is operating cash flow less purchases of property and equipment, or another explicitly reconciled measure. Include lease payments, capitalized development, acquisitions or financing costs only under the chosen convention; compare issuers consistently. Net debt likewise requires choices about leases, restricted cash, pensions and financial subsidiaries. Avoid mixing enterprise-value numerators with equity-only denominators.

Present a compact base case and two sensitivities for the assumptions that materially affect the conclusion. For example, show cash conversion under both reported and adjusted working-capital treatment. Distinguish descriptive observations from causal claims: a margin decline coinciding with expansion does not by itself establish that expansion caused it. Close the analysis with source dates, formulas, comparability breaks and unresolved reconciliation items, not a mechanically generated investment recommendation. Analytical reproducibility matters more than a long list of unverified ratios.
