# Portfolio risk and backtest integrity

Define the return series before estimating risk: simple or log returns, frequency, time zone, total-return treatment and base currency. Align calendars without inventing traded prices on closed-market days. Forward filling may be appropriate for marking a position but can bias volatility and correlations; state the method and run a sensitivity when asynchronous markets matter. Include delisted instruments where the research design requires them and use information available at the simulated decision time.

For portfolio weights `w` and covariance matrix `Σ` of compatible simple returns, variance is `wᵀΣw`. Check symmetry, finite entries and positive semidefiniteness within numerical tolerance. Pairwise deletion can produce an invalid covariance matrix. Do not silently clip negative eigenvalues without explaining the estimator and its impact. When observations are scarce relative to assets, compare a shrinkage or factor estimate with the sample estimate and report the stability of the result.

Annualizing daily volatility by `sqrt(N)` assumes an appropriate return process and calendar. Serial correlation or changing exposures can invalidate the shortcut. Historical simulation must revalue the current portfolio under historical risk-factor changes; it is not always equivalent to applying today's weights to historical instrument returns. Options and path-dependent exposures may need full repricing. Define the treatment of leverage, financing, cash, rebalance costs and transaction costs.

Use a positive-loss convention and state it. Value at Risk at confidence `α` is a loss quantile, not the worst possible loss. Expected Shortfall concerns the tail beyond the chosen quantile; finite samples and ties require an explicit estimator, including fractional quantile mass if used. Basel's market-risk framework uses expected-shortfall-based modeling in a regulatory setting; a personal or research report should not label a generic calculation as regulatory compliance. [BIS market-risk framework](https://www.bis.org/publications/201901-standards-minimum-capital-requirements-market-risk).

Show scenario loss alongside statistical estimates: parallel and nonparallel rate moves, currency depreciation, spread widening, equity drawdown, volatility changes and liquidity haircuts as relevant. Avoid adding mutually incompatible shocks without explanation. Report gross and net exposures, concentration and maximum historical drawdown, with drawdown measured from a running wealth peak. A portfolio can have low recent variance and substantial gap or liquidity risk.

Validate with held-out periods, rolling or expanding windows and explicit transaction timing. Separate parameter selection from final evaluation, and account for repeated strategy searches rather than presenting the best backtest as an unbiased estimate. Test a single-asset case, identical assets, offsetting positions and a constant-price series. Report uncertainty from short samples and unstable estimates; attach the exact data window and assumptions to every metric. Risk analysis supports a human decision and does not itself trigger trades.
