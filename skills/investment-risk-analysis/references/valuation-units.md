# Valuation contracts and dimensional checks

Represent a position using instrument identifier, quantity, quote convention, multiplier, quote currency, valuation timestamp and source. A bond price quoted per 100 par is not a currency amount per bond; an option contract may represent multiple shares. Record whether a bond quote is clean or includes accrued interest. Futures notional, initial margin and mark-to-market exposure are different quantities. Instrument documentation controls these conventions, so verify the actual contract specification instead of inferring it from a ticker.

Convert value to the chosen base currency using an explicit FX direction. If the quote is USD per EUR, a EUR value is multiplied by that quote to obtain USD. Store the conversion formula beside the source timestamp. Test that a unit position converts sensibly in both directions. For international return decomposition with compatible end-period FX rates, base-currency gross return equals local-currency gross return times FX gross return. Do not add the two returns and omit their product except as a stated small-change approximation.

Discount cash flow `CF_t` by a factor consistent with its timing and currency. For a flat annual effective rate and annual periods, `PV = sum(CF_t/(1+r)^t)`; actual instruments can require day counts, calendars, compounding and a full curve. A curve's zero rates are not automatically the same as par yields. Do not plug a monthly rate into annual exponents. Verify zero-rate and zero-cash-flow limits and ensure a bump in discount rates produces the expected direction for ordinary positive fixed cash flows.

For a simple perpetual growth terminal value, `TV_T = CF_(T+1)/(r-g)` requires `r > g` and a consistent cash-flow definition. Large terminal-value shares signal assumption sensitivity. Enterprise value discounted from unlevered cash flow must be bridged to equity using the stated debt, cash and non-operating adjustments; equity cash flows use an equity discount rate. Diluted share counts, options and minority interests can materially change per-share values.

For fixed income, distinguish modified duration from Macaulay duration and quantify the approximation range. A local second-order approximation is `ΔP/P ≈ -D_mod Δy + 0.5 C (Δy)^2`; yields are decimal changes, so 100 basis points equals 0.01. Use full repricing when cash flows depend on rates or shocks are large. Investor-facing bond risk includes more than interest-rate sensitivity, such as credit and liquidity risk. [FINRA bond guidance](https://www.finra.org/investors/investing/investment-products/bonds).

Prefer base, adverse and favorable scenarios with documented inputs to a single precise-looking target. For derivatives, disclose model family, exercise style, volatility input and dividend assumptions, and validate against a recognized implementation or independent limit case. A spreadsheet that calculates successfully is not evidence that its valuation conventions are correct. Label delayed, indicative or historical quotes and avoid presenting them as executable prices.
